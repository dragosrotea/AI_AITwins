# DriveMate – AI Driver Safety Copilot: Requirements Specification

Nov 20, 2026 · @Vlad

## 1. Overview

DriveMate is a voice-first copilot that watches the driver through a camera, listens in the cabin, and talks the driver back to safety when it detects anger, zoning out, distraction or fatigue. Every alert is spoken and must be answered by voice, so the driver never has to look at or touch the screen.

**Working name:** DriveMate (rename freely). **Team:** 4 engineers. **Build window:** CodeLap 2026, Fri 20 Nov evening to Sun 22 Nov afternoon (about 40 working hours).

### Problem

Most crashes trace back to human state, not the car: a few seconds of distraction, a microsleep on a monotonous highway, or an angry driver taking risks. Drivers rarely notice their own state changing, and phone apps that need taps make the problem worse.

### Product goals

1. **Detect** risky driver states live from the camera and microphone: road rage, zoning out (highway hypnosis), distraction and drowsiness.
2. **React** with short, context-aware spoken interventions generated by an LLM, and confirm the driver is alert through a natural-language voice reply.
3. **Prevent** fatigue proactively with check-ins scheduled from what the driver tells us before the trip.
4. **Show** everything live on a dashboard: driver state, acceleration, cabin audio and music volume, alerts and responses.
5. **Escalate** when the driver does not respond or a crash is detected (extra scope).

### Alignment with the CodeLap challenge

The organisers judge the demo against four hard requirements. Our feature list fits them, but only if detection is driven by computer vision and the reaction by an LLM. That is a design constraint on every feature below.

| CodeLap requirement | What it demands | How DriveMate meets it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 Driver detection | Computer vision reads driver activity and attention | Face landmarks give eye closure, gaze, head pose, yawns and angry expression; optional phone-in-hand detection |
| R2 In-car reaction | Alert, warning, dialogue or voice assist, using an LLM | LLM writes every spoken alert, trivia question and calming message, and interprets the driver's voice reply |
| R3 Live demo | Runs live on a real camera feed, no pre-labelled video | Live USB webcam + live microphone + live heart rate; vehicle motion replayed from a real recorded trip and labelled as such; terminal output acceptable as a fallback |
| R4 Live dashboard | All detections visible live, plus live text/audio suggestion | Web dashboard over WebSocket showing every signal, the attention score and the LLM's current message |
| Optional GenAI | Natural-language explanations, passenger interaction, RAG | Post-trip natural-language summary; voice reply understanding; trivia generation |

### Out of scope for the hackathon

- Native iOS/Android app. The prototype runs on a laptop with a USB webcam as the "in-car unit" and uses no phone; a mobile port stays possible later.
- Reading the car's CAN bus or OBD-II. Speed and acceleration are replayed from a real recorded trip (UAH-DriveSet) and shown as replay.
- Infrared night-vision camera. We document it as the production path and demo in good lighting.
- Placing a real call to 112 during the demo. The crash flow calls or texts a teammate's phone instead.
- Storing video or audio. Only derived signals and transcripts of voice replies are kept.

## 3. Users and scenarios

The primary user is a driver of a car without built-in driver monitoring; the secondary user is whoever reviews the trip afterwards (the driver, a parent or a fleet manager).

### Why this is realistic and impactful

The EU now requires driver drowsiness and distraction warnings in new cars: drowsiness warning (DDAW) since July 2024 and advanced distraction warning (ADDW) on new types since July 2024, extending to all new cars ([ETSC overview](https://etsc.eu/comparative-overview-eu-us-vehicle-standards/)). The cars already on the road have none of this. A camera-based retrofit (a webcam and laptop today, a small in-car unit later) brings the same protection to the existing fleet, which is our pitch.

US NHTSA guidance limits eyes-off-road time to 2 seconds per glance and 12 seconds per task ([US DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/us-dot-releases-guidelines-minimize-vehicle-distractions)). We use that as our distraction threshold and as a design rule for our own UI: nothing in DriveMate needs a glance while moving.

### Personas

| Persona | Context | What they need from DriveMate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Andrei, 34, commuter | 2 h motorway drive Bucharest to Brașov every Friday evening, often after a full workday | Catch zoning out and fatigue; suggest breaks at the right time |
| Ioana, 27, city driver | Rush-hour traffic, gets frustrated and aggressive | De-escalate anger early, without lecturing |
| Mihai, 45, delivery driver | 8 h shifts, many stops, a fleet manager reviews safety | Check-ins on a schedule, trip report, crash escalation |
| Fleet manager / parent | Reviews trips afterwards, never interacts while driving | Post-trip summary and event log |

### Key scenarios (used as acceptance tests and the demo script)

1. **Pre-trip setup.** Andrei enters trip length 2 h, 5 h of sleep, departure 21:00. DriveMate sets check-ins every 15 min and a break reminder at 1 h 45 min.
2. **Highway hypnosis.** After 25 min of steady speed his gaze becomes fixed and blink rate drops. DriveMate asks a trivia question; he answers by voice and the alertness score recovers.
3. **Microsleep.** His eyes close for more than 1 s. DriveMate skips trivia, sounds a tone, asks him to confirm he is awake and recommends the next rest area.
4. **Road rage.** Ioana frowns, swears and shouts after being cut off. DriveMate lowers the music, says one calm line and asks her to take a breath; she replies "ok, I'm fine".
5. **No response.** A driver ignores two prompts. DriveMate escalates to a continuous alarm and logs a critical event.
6. **Crash (extra).** A spike above the crash threshold is followed by a stop. DriveMate asks "Are you okay?" and starts a 20-second countdown; with no answer it calls the emergency contact with the GPS location.

## 4. System architecture

Sensing and detection run locally on the laptop: small models trained on public datasets read the driver's face and voice, and explainable rules combine their outputs; the LLM is used only for language (writing what to say and understanding the reply). Everything talks through one event bus, so the dashboard can show every signal live.

Signals flow top to bottom: inputs become features, detectors turn features into events, and the Alert Manager is the single place that decides what the driver hears.

### Alert lifecycle

1. A detector publishes an event (for example `road_rage`, score 72, tier T2).
2. The Alert Manager checks cooldowns, road load and the queue, then asks the LLM for the spoken text (template after 2 s).
3. Voice I/O lowers the music, speaks the message, and opens an 8 s listening window.
4. The reply is transcribed and classified (keyword fast path, then LLM): OK, NOT\_OK, ANSWER, HELP or UNCLEAR.
5. OK closes the alert. NOT\_OK triggers a follow-up (break offer). UNCLEAR or silence repeats once, then escalates a tier. HELP starts the emergency flow.
6. Every step is published to the bus, logged to the trip log and drawn on the dashboard.

## 5. Functional requirements

Each requirement has an ID the team can reference in commits and issues. Thresholds are starting values to calibrate on Saturday morning; all of them live in one config file, and a "demo mode" profile shortens the time windows so effects show within a 5-minute demo.

### 5.1 Alert model (applies to every feature)

All features raise events into one Alert Manager, which decides what is said, when, and how loudly. This keeps the driver from being talked at by five features at once.

| Tier | Examples | Delivery | Response required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Engage | Trivia, fun fact, scheduled check-in | Normal voice, music ducked | Yes, 8 s window |
| T2 Warning | Distraction > 2 s, drowsiness signs, road rage, phone in hand | Chime + firm voice, music lowered | Yes, 8 s window |
| T3 Critical | Microsleep, two missed responses, crash, "help" | Alarm tone + voice, music muted | Yes; silence escalates |

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| AL-01 | Only one prompt is spoken at a time; others queue by tier, and T3 pre-empts anything playing. |
| AL-02 | At most one T1 prompt per 5 min and one non-critical prompt per 2 min overall, to avoid alert fatigue. |
| AL-03 | When the road demands focus (X2 says HIGH load), T1 prompts are held until load drops; T2 and T3 are never held. |
| AL-04 | Every alert is logged with timestamp, trigger signals, spoken text, reply transcript, intent and response latency. |
| AL-05 | If the LLM does not answer within 2 s, a template message is spoken instead. Safety never waits on the network. |

### 5.2 F0 Core computer-vision pipeline (MUST)

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CV-01 | Read the driver-facing USB webcam (plugged into the laptop) live and process at least 15 frames per second on a team laptop CPU. | FPS counter on dashboard stays at or above 15 for 5 min. |
| CV-02 | Run a face-landmark model per frame (MediaPipe Face Landmarker: 478 landmarks, iris points and 52 expression blendshapes). | Landmarks drawn on the live preview track the face while the driver moves. |
| CV-03 | Compute per frame: eye aspect ratio (each eye), mouth aspect ratio, head yaw/pitch/roll, gaze direction, and expression scores. | Values visible live in the debug panel. |
| CV-04 | No manual calibration. Eye-state, yawn and expression models are trained on public datasets (section 5.15), so detection works on a new driver from the first frame. | Works for all 4 team members with no setup step; accuracy measured on test subjects the models never saw. |
| CV-05 | Keep rolling windows: PERCLOS (share of time eyes are closed) over 60 s, blink rate per minute, gaze dispersion over 30 s, yawn count over 10 min. | Unit tests on recorded landmark sequences. |
| CV-06 | If no face is found for 2 s, show "driver not visible"; after 5 s raise a T2 warning. | Covering the camera triggers the state, then the warning. |
| CV-07 | Publish a DriverState message on the event bus 10 times per second. | Dashboard and Alert Manager both consume it. |

### 5.3 F6 Distraction and drowsiness (MUST, added)

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DD-01 | Eyes off road: head yaw beyond 30° or pitch below −20° (looking down) for more than 2 s continuously raises T2. | Looking at the lap for 3 s triggers a warning within 2.5 s of looking away. |
| DD-02 | Microsleep: eyes closed for 1.0 s or more raises T3. | Closing eyes for 1.5 s triggers an alarm within 0.5 s of the threshold. |
| DD-03 | Drowsiness: PERCLOS at or above 0.15 over 60 s, or 3 or more yawns in 10 min, raises T2 and a break suggestion. | Demo-mode windows show it within 60 s of fake yawning. |
| DD-04 | A drowsiness or microsleep event always overrides trivia: tired drivers get a break recommendation, not a game. | Rule tested in the Alert Manager unit tests. |

### 5.4 F1 Road rage detection (MUST)

Road rage is fused from three independent signals so that one alone (singing along to a song with swearing, for example) does not trigger it.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RR-01 | Visual anger probability from a facial-expression model trained on a public dataset (FER2013), sustained for 3 s. | An exaggerated frown for 3 s raises the visual score above 0.6. |
| RR-02 | Swearing detection: on-device speech recognition of cabin audio, matched against a configurable Romanian + English profanity list. Counts per 60 s. | 10 test phrases with swear words are counted; 10 without are not. |
| RR-03 | Shouting detection: microphone loudness at least 15 dB above the cabin baseline for 1 s or longer; stretch goal, an angry-voice classifier trained on CREMA-D. | Shouting raises the flag; normal talking and music at constant volume do not. |
| RR-04 | Driving-style cue: harsh acceleration or braking above 0.4 g in the last 60 s adds weight. | A harsh-braking segment in the vehicle-data replay adds to the score. |
| RR-05 | Fusion: Rage Score 0–100. Trigger at 60 or above only when at least 2 of 3 modalities (face, voice, driving) agree, or the face alone stays angry for 5 s. At 85 or above use a firmer message and suggest pulling over when safe. | Frown + swear + shout triggers within 3 s; swearing alone never triggers. |
| RR-06 | Intervention: lower music volume by 30%, then the LLM says one calm, non-judgmental sentence (20 words maximum) and asks for a reply. | Message never blames or lectures; reviewed against 10 generated samples. |
| RR-07 | Cooldown of 3 min between rage interventions and at most 3 in 30 min. | Repeated anger inside the cooldown is logged but not spoken. |

### 5.5 F2 Zoning out with trivia and fun facts (MUST)

Zoning out (highway hypnosis, tunnel vision) looks different from drowsiness: eyes are open but the gaze is fixed and blinking slows. Light conversation helps here; it does not help a driver who is falling asleep, so the two paths are kept separate.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ZO-01 | Zoning-out score from: low gaze dispersion (fixed stare) for 60 s or more, blink rate at least 40% below baseline, very little head movement, and monotony context (steady speed above 70 km/h for 10 min or more, from the vehicle-data replay). | Fixed stare in demo mode for 30 s raises the score above threshold. |
| ZO-02 | If drowsiness signs are present (DD-02, DD-03), use the drowsiness path instead of trivia. | Unit-tested in the Alert Manager. |
| ZO-03 | The LLM generates either a fun fact (tied to the region or route when location is known) or a trivia question answerable in 1 to 3 words. No content needs the screen. | 20 generated items reviewed: all speakable in under 10 s. |
| ZO-04 | The driver's spoken answer is graded leniently by the LLM, with one short sentence of feedback. Response latency is logged as an alertness measure. | "Paris", "it's Paris" and "Parisul" all accepted. |
| ZO-05 | At most one engagement per 5 min. After 3 engagements in 30 min, stop the trivia and recommend a break: persistent zoning out means fatigue. | Fourth trigger produces a break recommendation. |
| ZO-06 | No trivia in high-load zones (X2) or in stop-and-go traffic. | Held prompts appear in the queue on the dashboard. |
| ZO-07 | Offline fallback bank of 50 pre-written questions and facts (JSON) for when the LLM is slow or unreachable. | Disconnecting Wi-Fi still produces a question. |

### 5.6 F3 Pre-trip input and personalised check-ins (MUST)

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CI-01 | Pre-trip form (touch or voice) captures: planned duration, hours slept last night, hours awake, self-rated tiredness 1–5, caffeine in the last 2 h, engagement preference (trivia / fun facts / quiet), language (RO/EN), emergency contact, resting heart rate. Departure time is read automatically. | Form completes in under 60 s; values shown on the dashboard. |
| CI-02 | Check-in interval rule: start at 30 min; halve it if sleep is under 6 h; halve it if tiredness is 4 or more; multiply by 0.66 when driving between 00:00–06:00 or 14:00–16:00 (circadian dips). Clamp between 10 and 45 min. | Unit tests: 5 h sleep, departure 21:00 gives 15 min. |
| CI-03 | Break reminder every 2 h of driving, brought forward to 1 h 45 min when any risk factor in CI-02 applies. | Scenario 1 schedules a break at 1:45. |
| CI-04 | The interval adapts live: each T2/T3 event in the last 15 min shortens the next check-in by 30%; 30 min without events lengthens it by 20%, up to the maximum. | Dashboard countdown changes after a triggered warning. |
| CI-05 | A check-in is a short LLM-generated question about the driver's state. A "tired" reply offers a break (and the nearest rest area if X2 is on) and shortens the interval. | "I'm a bit sleepy" leads to a break offer. |
| CI-06 | The setup form is locked while speed is above 5 km/h; changes while driving go by voice only. | Form disabled in moving state. |

### 5.7 F4 Mandatory voice response (MUST)

Every alert must be acknowledged by voice in natural language. This both confirms alertness and keeps the driver's hands on the wheel and eyes on the road.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VR-01 | Every alert ends with a question that needs a spoken reply. | Audit of all templates and LLM prompts. |
| VR-02 | The listening window opens when the spoken alert ends and lasts 8 s; music is ducked while listening. | Window visible on dashboard as a countdown. |
| VR-03 | Speech is transcribed, then classified into: OK ("yes", "sure", "I'm fine", "da, sunt ok"), NOT\_OK ("I'm tired", "not really"), ANSWER (trivia), HELP ("help", "call someone", "ajutor"), UNCLEAR. | 30 varied phrasings in RO and EN, 90% or more classified correctly. |
| VR-04 | Two-step classifier: a keyword fast path answers in under 100 ms; anything else goes to the LLM with a 1.5 s budget. | Latency logged per reply; median under 1.5 s end-to-end. |
| VR-05 | No reply or UNCLEAR: repeat once, shorter and louder. A second miss escalates one tier (T1 to T2, T2 to T3); a miss at T3 sounds a continuous alarm and, in a crash context, starts the emergency flow. | Staying silent through two prompts produces the alarm. |
| VR-06 | HELP at any time starts the emergency flow (X3) or, without X3, a spoken confirmation and logged critical event. | Saying "help" during a check-in triggers it. |
| VR-07 | Response latency above 3 s on two consecutive prompts raises the fatigue score. | Visible in the attention score breakdown. |

### 5.8 F5 Live dashboard (MUST)

The dashboard has two views. The **Driver view** is glanceable only: one colour status, the current spoken message in large text, nothing to tap while moving. The **Co-pilot view** shows everything live for a passenger, the jury and post-trip review.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DB-01 | Web dashboard fed over WebSocket, refreshing at least 5 times per second with under 300 ms from event to screen. | Measured with timestamps in the debug panel. |
| DB-02 | Acceleration: longitudinal and lateral g from the vehicle-data replay (real smartphone IMU recordings from UAH-DriveSet, labelled “replay” on screen), streamed at 10 Hz, plotted over the last 60 s with harsh-event markers above 0.4 g. | Injecting a harsh-brake event moves the chart within 0.5 s. |
| DB-03 | Music volume: the app's built-in music player reports its volume (0–100); system volume is read too where the OS allows. Cabin loudness from the microphone (dB) is plotted beside it. | Changing volume updates the chart within 0.5 s. |
| DB-04 | Emotional cues derived and shown as tags: volume rising together with anger signals = "stress"; low volume and low arousal on a monotonous road = "low engagement". Tags are context, not triggers. | Tags appear in the scripted road-rage scenario. |
| DB-05 | Driver state panel: attention score 0–100 plus its parts (drowsiness, distraction, rage, zoning out), eyes/gaze/head indicators, live camera preview with optional landmark overlay. | All CV detections visible live (R4). |
| DB-06 | Conversation panel: current LLM message (text, with an audio-playing indicator), the driver's transcribed reply, classified intent and latency. | Matches what is heard in the room. |
| DB-07 | Timeline of all events, next check-in countdown, trip time and speed; plus heart rate, road load level and crash status when extras are on. | Every alert in AL-04 appears on the timeline. |

### 5.10 LLM behaviour (applies to F1–F4)

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| LLM-01 | The LLM receives a compact JSON context (event type, tier, signal values, trip time, driver preferences, language) and returns the spoken text only. |

### 5.15 Models and datasets (replaces manual calibration)

Each perception task uses a model trained or tuned on a public dataset and tested on people it never saw, so the driver never has to calibrate anything. We train on compact features (eye crops, mouth and face blendshapes, audio embeddings) rather than raw video, so training fits in a few hours on a laptop.

| Task | Dataset | Model and use | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eyes open / closed (microsleep, PERCLOS) | [MRL Eye Dataset](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22479): 84,898 eye images, open and closed, varied lighting | Small CNN on grayscale eye crops cut out with MediaPipe landmarks; outputs eye-closed probability, replacing the eye-ratio threshold | CV |
| Yawning vs talking or singing | [YawDD](https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/yawdd-yawning-detection-dataset): in-car videos of drivers silent, talking or singing, and yawning, with and without glasses | Classifier on 2 s windows of mouth features; keeps the driver's spoken replies from counting as yawns | CV |
| Anger expression | FER2013 (7 emotions) | Pretrained expression model trained on it, checked on its held-out test split | CV |
| Distraction, gaze and drowsiness evaluation | [DMD](https://dmd.vicomtech.org/): 41 h of video of 37 drivers covering distraction, gaze, drowsiness ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12085)); academic and scientific use | Test set and threshold tuning for head-pose distraction; too large to train on in 48 h | CV |
| Vehicle data and driving style | [UAH-DriveSet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.04794): 6 drivers, normal, drowsy and aggressive driving on motorway and secondary roads, smartphone motion and GPS | Replayed as the car's live data stream; harsh-event thresholds tuned on it | Lead |
| Angry voice (stretch) | CREMA-D (acted emotional speech) | Small classifier on audio embeddings, adds to shouting detection | Voice |
| Phone in hand | COCO (pretrained YOLOv8n) | Used as is | CV |

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| ML-01 | No calibration step at trip start; detection works on a new driver from the first frame. |
| ML-02 | Every model reports precision and recall on a held-out set of people not used in training; the numbers go in the README and the deck. |
| ML-03 | Training scripts live in `training/`, small trained weights in `models/`; datasets are never committed (size and licences), only a download script. |
| ML-04 | Each dataset's licence is checked and named in the README; most allow research and academic use only. DMD may need a registration request, so request it tonight and drop it if access has not arrived by Sat 10:00. |
| ML-05 | Domain check: run each model on 2 min of our own webcam footage of each team member. If accuracy drops, adjust the shared decision threshold on that validation set, never per driver. |

## 6. Non-functional requirements

The safety-critical path (camera to alarm) must run locally, fast, and without the internet; the LLM only makes the experience better.

| ID | Category | Requirement | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NF-01 | Latency | Critical detection (microsleep) to alarm sound | Under 500 ms |
| NF-02 | Latency | Warning detection to start of spoken message (LLM path) | Under 2.5 s; template fallback after 2 s |
| NF-03 | Latency | End of driver reply to classified intent | Median under 1.5 s |
| NF-04 | Performance | CV pipeline and models together on a mid-range laptop CPU | 15 FPS or more, CPU under 70% |
| NF-05 | Reliability | Works with the venue Wi-Fi down | CV, alarms, templates and offline trivia keep working |
| NF-06 | Reliability | One component crashing (e.g. speech) does not stop the others | Each module a separate process or task, auto-restart |
| NF-07 | Distraction-safe UX | No interaction needs a glance longer than 2 s while moving; nothing to tap above 5 km/h | Design review against every screen |
| NF-08 | Accuracy | False alarm rate in a 10-min normal "driving" session at the desk | 1 or fewer T2 alerts |
| NF-09 | Accuracy | Scripted risky behaviours detected | 9 of 10 or better per scenario |
| NF-10 | Robustness | Glasses, different skin tones, moderate head movement, no calibration | Tested on all 4 team members |
| NF-11 | Language | Voice prompts and replies in Romanian and English | Selected at setup |
| NF-12 | Maintainability | Thresholds, prompts and lexicons in config files, not code | One config file per module |
| NF-13 | Observability | Every event and LLM call logged to a JSONL trip log | Replayable for debugging and the post-trip summary |
| NF-14 | Generalisation | Models tested on people not seen in training | Eye-closed recall 90% or more; every model's precision and recall reported |
| NF-15 | Training budget | Each model trains on team hardware or a free GPU notebook | Under 2 h per model |

### Privacy and data handling

A camera and an always-listening microphone in a car are sensitive, so privacy is part of the design, not an afterthought. It is also an easy question for the jury to ask.

- **No raw media leaves the device or is stored.** Frames are processed in memory; only derived numbers (eye ratio, gaze angles, scores) are kept.
- **Speech recognition for swear detection runs on the device** (offline model) and keeps only counts, not transcripts. Transcripts are kept only for replies to alerts.
- **Only short text goes to the LLM**: event type, scores, the reply transcript. No images, no audio, no name.
- **Consent at setup**: the driver sees what is monitored and can turn off audio monitoring (road rage then uses face and driving signals only).
- **Trip logs stay local** and can be deleted from the app. A fleet version would need explicit employee consent under GDPR.
- **Safety disclaimer**: DriveMate assists and does not replace the driver's responsibility; it never controls the vehicle.
